About HEART™

Five human abilities we want children to practice on purpose.

HEART™ is GrowMate™'s competency framework for observing growth across projects, group work, presentations, and reflection.

Why These Five

A practical framework for human development

HEART™ draws on the OECD Learning Compass 2030 and the P21 Framework for 21st Century Learning. We use those ideas as a starting point, then organize them around the behaviors we can actually observe in GrowMate™ projects.

The question behind the framework is simple: when AI can handle more information and more routine tasks, what do children still need to practice themselves?

Our answer is five areas: collaboration, communication, action, resilience, and thinking. Each area is broken into six development indicators, for 30 indicators in total.

HEART™

The five competencies

H

Human Collaboration

Work with people, not just beside them.

Build connections, take on a role, read the group, work through disagreement, and take responsibility for shared progress.

What we look for: trust-building, participation, perspective-taking, conflict navigation, and shared responsibility.

E

Expression & Communication

Make an idea understandable.

Express ideas clearly, understand other people, ask when something is unclear, respond well, and adjust to the audience or situation.

What we look for: clarity, listening, questions, response, and audience awareness.

A

Action & Problem Solving

Turn an idea into progress.

Start, define the problem, plan a path, follow through, adjust when needed, and own the result.

What we look for: initiative, problem definition, planning, follow-through, iteration, and ownership.

R

Resilience & Self-management

Keep working when things get uncomfortable.

Manage emotions under pressure, stay steady through difficulty, recover from setbacks, and build habits that support independent work.

What we look for: regulation, persistence, recovery, focus, and growing independence.

T

Thinking & Creativity

Ask better questions and make better judgments.

Ask useful questions, reason from evidence, test assumptions, look from another angle, and combine ideas into something new.

What we look for: questioning, evidence, logic, perspective shifts, originality, and synthesis.
