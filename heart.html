About HEART™

HEART™ is GrowMate™'s proprietary competency framework — representing the five Human Core Competencies children need most, and that AI is least equipped to replace, in the AI era.

Why These Five

HEART™ is built on two of the most respected international frameworks for 21st-century learning: the OECD Learning Compass 2030 and the P21 Framework for 21st Century Learning. It was developed in direct response to what the AI era actually demands of human beings — and represents one of GrowMate™'s core intellectual assets.

We started with a question: when AI can memorize everything and execute any standardized task, what does a child actually need?

The answer is five capabilities. They cannot be replicated by algorithms. They will not be made obsolete by technology. And they compound across every stage of a child's life — from the classroom to the workplace, from personal relationships to civic life.
HEART™ Five Human Core Competencies

Each one irreplaceable. All five essential.

H
Human Collaboration
Actively building connections, taking on roles, reading others, navigating disagreement, holding collective responsibility, and moving the team forward.
AI can handle your information and organize your meeting notes — but it cannot make your teammates trust you, and it cannot hold a team together when things get hard.
Children who can sense others, work through conflict, and move forward together possess a competitive edge AI will never be able to replicate.
E
Expression & Communication
Expressing ideas clearly, understanding others accurately, seeking clarification proactively, responding effectively, and adjusting communication style for different people and contexts.
AI can polish your writing and generate your content — but it cannot give you a point of view, and it cannot move an audience the way a human voice can.
Children who communicate with clarity and intention don't just present themselves better — they influence people and change the world around them.
A
Action & Problem Solving
Turning goals into action: initiating, defining the problem, planning a path, following through, iterating, and taking ownership of outcomes.
AI can generate options and analyze scenarios — but it cannot make the real decision, and it cannot be held responsible for what happens next.
Children who can identify what actually matters, take initiative, and own their results are the ones who use AI as a tool — never the other way around.
R
Resilience & Self-management
Regulating emotions under pressure, maintaining stability through difficulty, recovering from setbacks, and developing increasingly mature self-management habits.
AI can manage your calendar and set your reminders — but it cannot keep you clear-headed under pressure, and it cannot get back up after you fall.
Children who stay grounded in uncertainty and recover from failure turn every wave of technological change into opportunity — not threat.
T
Thinking & Creativity
Asking meaningful questions, making reasoned judgments, applying logical thinking, shifting perspectives, generating original ideas, and synthesizing different viewpoints into something new.
AI can give you answers — but it cannot ask the questions that matter, and it cannot decide what is worth pursuing in the first place.
Children who think independently, judge critically, and solve problems creatively are the ones who truly direct AI — knowing what to ask, what to trust, and what to push beyond.
