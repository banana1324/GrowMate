Our Program

GrowMate™ HEART PBL Program

We don't hold classes. We make real experience the classroom.
We don't teach abilities. We let them grow through challenge.
We don't chase grades. We're in it for the long game.
What It Is

When your child joins GrowMate™, they're matched into a group of four same-age peers. Each month, the group works through one complete PBL project centered on a real driving question — guided by a Role Model Mentor through the full GrowLoop™ Project Growth Cycle: from inquiry and design, through creating and revising, to presenting their work in front of peers and family, and reflecting on the whole experience.

This isn't a class. It's a system built for real growth.

What Children Actually Gain
You might be wondering: how is this different from a class? What does my child actually get out of it?
Not knowledge — capability. Not knowing — doing. Not learning — practicing.
Here's what children are actually building at every step of the GrowLoop™ Project Growth Cycle —
①
Learning to look at the world with questions
Inquire
Every project starts with a real driving question. Children have to find their own answers — researching, observing, interviewing, and comparing sources. What they're building is the instinct to ask good questions and the discipline to seek out real evidence — and most importantly, the habit of not accepting the first answer they find.

In a world where AI can generate an answer to anything in seconds, knowing what to ask is worth more than knowing what the answer is.
HEART™: T · Thinking & Creativity · A · Action & Problem Solving
②
Learning to make choices and own them
Design
After inquiry, children have to decide: how am I going to present what I found? No one tells them what to do — they make the call, and then build a plan around it.

Making your own decisions and being accountable for what comes next is something many children rarely get to practice in a meaningful way. This step builds exactly that: the ability to plan with intention, and the willingness to stand behind your choices.
HEART™: A · Action & Problem Solving · T · Thinking & Creativity
③
Building capability through real doing
Create
When children actually start making something, they discover that the first idea doesn't always work, the plan doesn't always hold, and progress requires adjusting, trying again, and adjusting once more. This isn't failure — it's what real creative work feels like. What children build here is the follow-through to turn an idea into something real, and the resilience to keep going when it gets hard.
HEART™: A · Action & Problem Solving · R · Resilience & Self-management
④
Learning to take feedback and get better
Revise
Children bring their draft to the group, and their peers respond with genuine curiosity — not polite praise, but real questions. Children have to listen, decide what's worth taking on board, and then actually go back and improve their work.

Receiving feedback without getting defensive, and incorporating input without losing your own point of view — this is a skill that takes consistent, deliberate practice to build. And GrowMate™ children practice it every single month.
HEART™: H · Human Collaboration · T · Thinking & Creativity · R · Resilience & Self-management
⑤
Speaking clearly in front of a real audience
Present
Children present their finished work to their peer group and family: what did I explore? How did I approach it? Why did I make the choices I made? They take real questions from a real audience.

The difference at GrowMate™ is that children do this every single month — building the kind of fluency that only comes from repetition in front of real audiences.
HEART™: E · Expression & Communication · H · Human Collaboration
⑥
Seeing your own growth, building your own drive
Reflect
After the presentation, children answer three questions: What am I most proud of this month? What was my biggest challenge, and how did I handle it? What did I learn from my peers?

This isn't a writing exercise. It's a practice of extracting real learning from real experience — learning how to learn. This meta-skill takes deliberate, repeated practice to develop — and it may be the most durable competitive advantage a child can build.
HEART™: R · Resilience & Self-management · T · Thinking & Creativity

At the end of every month, what children walk away with isn't a certificate. It's a real experience — and the genuine capability that grew from it.

This isn't a class. It's a system for building capability that lasts — and a thinking framework children will carry for life.
Three Pillars

Three Pillars. All Essential.

🔨
Real Projects
Each month, one real driving question. Children explore a genuine topic, move through the full GrowLoop™ Project Growth Cycle, and turn their thinking into a finished piece of work. No single right answer — just real choices, real challenges, and real results.
GrowMate™'s project library spans eight knowledge domains: Science & Nature, Technology & Media, Math & Data, Society & People, History & Literature, Arts & Expression, Entrepreneurship, and Critical Thinking. Every project naturally integrates multiple disciplines.
👥
Peer Groups
Every child belongs to a group of four same-age peers. They meet weekly, share progress, and come together at the Monthly Showcase to present their work and respond to each other.
Every month, children across the GrowMate™ community are working on the same driving question — each group approaching it in their own way. At the Monthly Showcase, everyone brings their work to the same stage.
🧭
Role Model Mentors
GrowMate™ Role Model Mentors don't lecture. They accompany children through discovery, creation, and reflection — asking the right questions, offering encouragement, guiding the debrief.
Their role shifts across the cycle: in Inquire, they spark curiosity without giving answers; in Create, they stay close and offer just enough support to keep things moving; in Present, they step back entirely and let the child own the stage.
Who It's For

Three Learner Levels

GrowMate™ serves children ages 6 to 17 across three learner levels. Children start where they are and move up naturally over time — the platform grows with them.

🔭
Explorers
探索者
Ages 6–9
The curious ones — learning through sensation and instinct, growing between structure and play
Children at this age think in concrete, tangible terms and learn best through hands-on experience. They're eager for positive feedback and just beginning to navigate collaboration and sharing in small groups.
Projects are clear in structure, close to everyday life, and open-ended — there's no single right answer, only real discoveries. Role Model Mentors provide enough scaffolding to keep children moving, and enough space for them to surprise themselves.
Sample projects: Where Does Our Trash Go? · A Story from Our Family
Parent role: Companion and observer — supporting weekly check-ins, submitting parent observations that feed directly into the child's growth record.
🏗
Builders
建造者
Ages 10–13
Ready to build something real — logic and feeling in parallel, beginning to care about what they're actually capable of
Children at this age are developing rapidly in abstract thinking, care deeply about peer relationships, and want their ideas taken seriously.
Projects extend beyond the home into the community and toward real audiences — giving children the experience of making something that actually matters to someone. Role Model Mentors operate more like coaches.
Sample projects: Our Community's International Culture Festival · Where Does My Favorite Food Come From?
Parent role: Supporter, stepping back — understanding the project goals, providing resources when needed, and handing full ownership of the work to the child.
🚀
Innovators
创新者
Ages 14–17
Ready to engage with the world — young people with conviction, empathy, and the capacity for real impact
Children at this age think with near-adult sophistication, hold strong ideals, and want to be trusted with real responsibility. Many high schoolers lack space for genuine self-directed exploration — GrowMate™ provides it.
Projects address city-scale and global questions, with pathways to share work in real public spaces. Role Model Mentors step into an advisory role — providing frameworks and feedback at key decision points, while leaving the direction entirely in the child's hands.
Sample projects: Education Equity in the Age of AI · How My City Has Changed
Parent role: Witness, largely stepping out — emotional support and broad-direction presence, without involvement in the project itself.

At the end of every month, parents receive a HEART™ Growth Report grounded in specific behavioral observation — not "Collaboration score: +5," but "This month, during the Create phase, your child proactively invited a peer to give feedback on their work — a clear example of H-1 · Proactive Connection." Concrete. Specific. Real.
Each month, a new driving question arrives. The thinking, creating, and growing continue — month after month.
