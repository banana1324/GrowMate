Our Program

GrowMate™ HEART PBL Program

We don't hold classes. We make real experience the classroom.

We don't teach abilities. We let them grow through challenge.

We don't define a child by where they are today. We care how far they can go.

What It Is

One small group. One real project each month.

Children are matched with three same-age peers to form a group of four. Each month, the group takes on one complete PBL project built around a real driving question.

A Role Model Mentor guides them through the GrowLoop™ cycle as they investigate, make decisions, create, revise, present their work to a real audience, and reflect on the experience.

What Children Actually Gain

The GrowLoop™ Project Growth Cycle

The same six steps repeat across different projects. That repetition is intentional: over time, children are not only completing projects; they are building a repeatable way to approach unfamiliar problems.

01

Inquire

Learning to look at the world with questions

Children research, observe, interview, compare sources, and decide what evidence they trust. They practice asking better questions, looking beyond the first easy answer, and finding evidence for themselves.

HEART™: Thinking & Creativity · Action & Problem Solving

Design

Learning to make choices and own them

Children decide how to approach the project, choose a direction, and build a workable plan. They practice making decisions with intention and taking responsibility for what comes next.

Create

Building capability through real doing

Plans meet reality. Children make, test, adjust, and keep going when the first version does not work as expected. They practice turning ideas into action, adapting, and following through.

04

Revise

Learning to use feedback without losing ownership

Peers ask questions and give real feedback. Children decide what is useful, what to change, and what to keep, improving the work while holding on to their own point of view.

Speaking clearly to a real audience

Three Pillars

Three Pillars. All Essential.

🔨

Real Projects

Each month begins with one real driving question. Children investigate, make choices, create, revise, present, and reflect, turning their thinking into a finished piece of work.

Projects cross subject boundaries, drawing from areas such as science, technology, society, history, arts, entrepreneurship, and critical thinking. Children learn to connect knowledge rather than treat subjects in isolation.

👥

Peer Groups

Four same-age children work through the same monthly challenge together. They share progress, divide responsibilities, respond to one another’s ideas, and present their work at the Monthly Showcase.

Peers are not competitors. They become collaborators, motivators, and witnesses to one another’s growth.

🧭

Role Model Mentors

Role Model Mentors guide without taking over. They ask questions, offer encouragement, observe how children approach the work, and give just enough support to keep them moving.

Their role changes across the GrowLoop™ cycle: they spark curiosity early, stay close while children create, and step back as children present, reflect, and take greater ownership.

Who It's For

Three Learner Levels

GrowMate™ serves children ages 6–17 across three learner levels. As children grow, projects become broader, decisions more open-ended, and mentors gradually step back as children take greater ownership.

🔭 Explorers

Ages 6–9

Hands-on, curious, and learning through experience.

Children at this stage learn best through concrete, hands-on experiences and clear structure. They are also beginning to build the habits of listening, sharing, taking turns, and working with others.

Projects stay close to everyday life, with clear steps and plenty of room for discovery. Role Model Mentors provide more guidance at this level while leaving space for children to make their own choices.

Sample projects: Where Does Our Trash Go? · A Story from Our Family

Parent role: Companion and observer. Help with logistics, notice effort and behavior, and give the child room to own the work.

🏗 Builders

Ages 10–13

More ownership, stronger peer influence, and wider questions.

Children at this stage are becoming more comfortable with abstract ideas, care more about peer relationships, and want their opinions and abilities to be taken seriously.

Projects begin to move beyond everyday life and into the community, with more open-ended decisions and real audiences. Role Model Mentors act more like coaches, pushing the thinking deeper without solving the problem for the child.

Sample projects: Our Community's International Culture Festival · Where Does My Favorite Food Come From?

Parent role: Supporter. Provide resources when needed, then step back and let the child take greater ownership.

🚀 Innovators

Ages 14–17

Greater independence, larger questions, and real responsibility.

Young people at this stage are ready to take on more complex questions, develop their own positions, and take greater responsibility for the direction and quality of their work.

Projects can address city-scale or global issues and involve real audiences beyond the immediate group. Role Model Mentors move into an advisory role, offering questions, frameworks, and feedback while leaving the direction increasingly in the student's hands.

Sample projects: Education Equity in the Age of AI · How My City Has Changed

Parent role: Witness. Offer emotional support and perspective without managing the project.

How Growth Becomes Visible

A record of growth built from real evidence

💧⭐🏅 Rewards & Milestones

Water Drops, Stars, and HEART™ badges recognize participation, optional challenges, and competency milestones. Over time, they create a visible record of effort and growth.

📁 My Profile

Project work, mentor notes, reflections, and earned milestones build into a personal growth archive that becomes richer over time.

🎤 Monthly Showcase

Each month, children present their work to peers and family, explain their thinking, answer questions, and complete the Reflect step. The showcase gives growth a real audience and a recurring moment to be seen.

📊 HEART™ Growth Report

Parents receive specific observations grounded in real behavior across projects and group work. The report shows what happened, how the child responded, and where growth is emerging, rather than reducing development to a generic score.

Growth compounds with practice.

Each month brings a new question, a new challenge, and another chance to practice the same way of thinking and doing.

Each cycle builds on the last, so growth compounds over time into capability children can carry into whatever comes next.
